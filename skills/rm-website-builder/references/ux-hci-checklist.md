# UI/UX, HCI, and Design-Law Checklist

Use this checklist when designing, implementing, or reviewing RM Group Strategies LLC website pages.

## Cognitive overload prevention

- Keep each section focused on one message.
- Use one primary CTA per section; use secondary CTAs sparingly.
- Prefer short paragraphs, bullet lists, cards, and visual grouping.
- Split dense government-contracting information into tabs, cards, accordions, or grouped sections if needed.
- Keep page navigation predictable and consistent.
- Use repeated layouts for division pages so users do not relearn the interface.
- Show only necessary form fields for the selected inquiry type.
- Provide inline validation and clear error summaries.

## HCI checklist

- Make interactive controls visually obvious.
- Ensure buttons and links have clear labels, not vague labels like `Click Here`.
- Use visible focus states for keyboard users.
- Keep tap targets comfortable on mobile.
- Maintain readable font sizes and line lengths.
- Preserve strong hierarchy: page title, section heading, body copy, CTA.
- Use labels, helper text, and error text on forms.
- Provide feedback after submit actions.
- Avoid unexpected navigation or layout shifts.

## Accessibility checklist

- Use semantic landmarks and heading order.
- Ensure color contrast works for black, gold, dark gray, and white.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Add meaningful `alt` text for informative images.
- Mark decorative images as decorative.
- Associate form labels with inputs.
- Use descriptive error messages.
- Support keyboard navigation.

## Design laws

### Hick's Law

Limit the number of choices in navigation, hero CTAs, and forms. If many options are required, group them into clear categories.

### Fitts's Law

Make primary buttons large and easy to click or tap. Place CTAs close to the content that explains them.

### Jakob's Law

Use familiar patterns for corporate websites: top navigation, hero, service cards, trust sections, contact CTAs, and footer.

### Miller's Law

Chunk long capability or service lists into smaller groups. Avoid walls of bullets.

### Gestalt laws

Use proximity, similarity, and common region to group related service cards, capabilities, forms, and contact details.

### Serial position effect

Place the most important services, credibility signals, and contact options at the beginning and end of pages.

### Peak-end rule

End each major page with a confident CTA and a clear reason to contact RM Group Strategies LLC.

### Tesler's Law

Do not hide unavoidable government procurement complexity, but structure it into clear, scannable sections.

### Aesthetic-usability effect

Use strong spacing, visual hierarchy, gold accents, high-quality imagery, and consistent components to make the site feel credible and easier to use.
